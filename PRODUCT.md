# Product

## Register

brand (default); product for /products/* routes

## Users

Two audiences sharing the same surface:

**Impact-driven organizations** (NGOs, social enterprises, community initiatives in South Asia and beyond) — looking for a design partner that understands their mission without reducing it to marketing. They want proof that the studio has worked in complex, high-stakes contexts before.

**Startups and technology companies** — looking for brand identity, digital presence, and product design from a studio with genuine aesthetic conviction. They don't want a vendor; they want a collaborator with a point of view.

Both audiences approach the site as evaluators. They're deciding whether Studio 1947's sensibility matches theirs. The site has one job: make them feel like this studio understands something that other studios don't.

## Product Purpose

Studio 1947 exists to show that design rooted in local knowledge can operate at global scale, without abandoning specificity for the sake of legibility. The studio's website is its most complete argument: it demonstrates the studio's craft, communicates its values, and makes the case that grounded, purposeful design produces better outcomes.

Success means a visitor understands what the studio believes and feels capable of making a decision, not just learning what services are offered.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, Purposeful, Unexpected.

**Voice**: Direct, assured, specific. Never vague or aspirational in the shallow sense. The studio has opinions; the writing and design should show them. Warmth comes from specificity, not softness.

**Tone**: Serious about impact without being solemn. Confident without being cold. The studio is based in Mirik, Darjeeling — that's specific, not incidental. Specificity is the brand.

**Emotional goal**: Visitors should feel they've encountered a studio with genuine conviction, not one performing it.

## Anti-references

- **Webflow template aesthetic**: rounded cards, friendly illustrations, "Modern. Fast. Scalable." copy, gradient CTAs, testimonial carousels. The comfortable default. Studio 1947 is not a vendor.
- **Linear/Stripe minimalism**: immaculate whitespace, single-accent, system-font precision. Too restrained; implies the design serves a product rather than being the product.
- **Generic agency portfolio**: identical case study cards, "We transform brands" headlines, award-badge rows, standardized process diagrams.
- **Consultant-corporate whitepaper aesthetic**: Futurice and Thoughtworks are references for mission and seriousness, not for visual language. Avoid sans-serif corporatism, blue-and-white earnestness, slide-deck layouts.

## Design Principles

1. **Specificity is the brand.** Every design decision — the image chosen, the copy written, the typeface used — should feel chosen for this studio, this context, this moment. Generic choices are a credibility problem, not a neutral fallback.

2. **Surprise once per surface.** Each page earns one unexpected choice: a layout break, a typographic scale jump, an image treatment that stops the scroll. More than one and the surprise becomes wallpaper.

3. **Work speaks; process is secondary.** Case studies lead with outcomes and material reality. The design question is "what did this change?" not "how did we think about it."

4. **Social mission is structural, not decorative.** Impact framing is embedded in how projects are described, how clients are introduced, how success is measured. Don't compartmentalize what should be foundational.

5. **The product pages are a different register.** /products/* (Doptor, Angan, etc.) serve users evaluating a tool, not evaluating a studio. These surfaces apply product-register design principles: clarity, hierarchy, task-orientation. They coexist with the brand register without inheriting its maximalism.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance as standard. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animations (Framer Motion, GSAP). Ensure sufficient contrast for the red (#D80000) brand color on both light and dark backgrounds. Bengali-language content should carry appropriate `lang` attributes and legible font fallbacks.
